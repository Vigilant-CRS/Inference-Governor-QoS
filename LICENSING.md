# Licensing

Short version: **testing is free, running your business on it is not.**

## What you may do without paying us anything

| | |
|---|---|
| Evaluate, develop, test, benchmark, research, teach, run in CI | **Free, no time limit** — including inside a company, and on production-grade hardware. We would rather you tested it properly than briefly. |
| Run it in production on **up to 3 devices** | **Free.** A pilot cell, a demo robot, a single line does not need a contract. |
| Read, modify, fork, publish patches | **Free.** It is source-available; the licence restricts *use in production*, not reading or changing. |
| Use any version four years after its release | **Free, under Apache-2.0.** Every version converts automatically. |

## What needs a commercial licence

- Production on **more than three devices** at a time.
- Shipping it **inside a product** you deliver to someone else.
- **Operating it for a third party** — as a service, or as part of one.

"Production" means the system is doing your organisation's actual work, or your
customers' work. A test rig doing the same job under evaluation is not
production, even if it is a company test rig on production hardware.

If you are not sure which side of that line you are on: **ask us before you
deploy.** We would rather answer than argue about it afterwards.

### Why a pilot first, and why it is not free

We have measured this on **one machine**. Your hardware, your models and your
load are not that machine, and we would be selling you a number we have not
taken. The pilot is where we take it — on your hardware, against criteria you
set, with a written result whether or not it flatters us.

If the pilot misses the criteria we agreed, you owe nothing beyond the pilot
fee and you keep the measurement report. That report is worth having either
way: it tells you what your GPU actually does under your load.

### How to think about the per-device price

If the governor lets a robot stay one Jetson tier lower, that is roughly
€700–900 of module cost per unit. €15 per month is a small share of that, and
you can stop paying it.

If it does not save you a hardware tier and your GPU is not saturated, you
should not buy it at all — see [when this helps and when it does
not](README.md#when-this-helps--and-when-it-does-not) in the README. We would
rather tell you that now than after an invoice.
